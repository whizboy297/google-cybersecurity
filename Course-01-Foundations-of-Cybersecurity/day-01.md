## Key Concepts

- Cybersecurity protects networks, devices, people, and data from cyber threats.
- The CIA Triad is the foundation of information security:
  - Confidentiality
  - Integrity
  - Availability
- Entry-level security analysts monitor systems, protect information, and search for vulnerabilities.
- Security audits evaluate an organization's security controls, policies, and records.
- Transferable skills (analysis, communication, problem-solving) are just as important as technical skills.
- Technical skills include programming, data analysis, networking, and security tools.
- Digital forensic investigators collect, analyze, and preserve digital evidence.
- Sensitive Personally Identifiable Information (SPII) requires stronger protection than regular PII.
- Organizations invest in cybersecurity to protect data, reduce risks, maintain customer trust, and comply with regulations.
- Phishing attacks often combine psychological manipulation with technical deception.

## My Notes

- Always verify the sender's email domain before trusting an email.
- Hover over links to check the actual destination before clicking.
- Technical indicators like suspicious domains and malicious URLs are stronger evidence of phishing than grammar mistakes alone.
- Security analysts constantly monitor systems for suspicious activity.
- Protecting information is not just about technology but also about protecting people and business operations.
- Availability means authorized users should always be able to access systems when needed.
- Integrity ensures that data remains accurate and unaltered.
- Confidentiality ensures that only authorized individuals can access sensitive information.
- Security is a continuous process of identifying, preventing, detecting, and responding to threats.
